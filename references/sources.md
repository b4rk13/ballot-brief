# Sources and ballot-resolution resources (US)

## Step 1: Resolve the official ballot
- **County or local election office:** most offer a "sample ballot" or "my
  ballot" lookup by name or address. This is authoritative.
- **State election office:** find yours via https://www.vote.gov or
  https://www.usa.gov/state-election-office
- Cross-check only (not authoritative): Ballotpedia sample ballot lookup,
  VOTE411 (League of Women Voters).

## Candidate records and positions
- **Ballotpedia:** candidate pages, survey responses, district info.
- Candidate campaign sites and social media (positions, endorsements).
- Legislative voting records: congress.gov, govtrack.us, state legislature
  sites, VoteSmart (votesmart.org).
- Local and state press; state public-radio newsrooms; AP.
- Voter guides from multiple outlets across the spectrum where available.

## Campaign finance
- **FEC.gov:** federal races (House, Senate, President).
- **OpenSecrets:** federal donor and industry breakdowns.
- **State elections division / ethics commission:** state and many local races.
- **FollowTheMoney / Transparency USA:** state-level money.
- **County election office:** local and judicial filings.
- Ad-spending trackers and super PAC filings (FEC independent expenditures).

## Ratings and polls
- Cook Political Report, Sabato's Crystal Ball, Inside Elections.
- Polling averages and individual polls; always note the sponsor, dates and
  sample. Treat campaign internal polls as partisan sources.

## Ballot measures
- State official fiscal-impact estimates (legislative budget office or
  financial impact conference), the official ballot text, Ballotpedia measure
  pages, nonpartisan tax and budget research groups, and the campaign committees
  on each side (for money).

## Redistricting
- Confirm current district lines every cycle; don't trust prior maps. The
  official sample ballot settles it.

## Reliability notes
- Paywalls and blocked databases are common; corroborate dollar figures from
  official filings when it matters, and note report dates.
- Prefer primary sources (filings, court records, official texts) over
  summaries. Mark party, campaign and advocacy-group materials as such.
- End every deliverable with a Sources list of the specific links used.
